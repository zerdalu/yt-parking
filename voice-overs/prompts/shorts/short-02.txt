[curious] Why doesn't the width of your own car appear anywhere in the mathematics of parallel parking? ...

[conversational] Look at mathematician Simon Blackburn's exact parking formula. It uses your turning radius... your wheelbase... your front overhang... and the width of the parked car in front of you. ...

[thoughtful] Your own car's width? Completely missing. ...

[confident] Here is why. In a single smooth sweep on a wide road... the only pinch point is your kerb-side front corner barely missing the car in front. ...

Making your own car wider pushes the outside edge closer to your turning center — without changing that front corner's path at all. ...

[conversational] In real life, of course... a wider car still steals room on narrow streets — or when you have to shuffle back and forth.
